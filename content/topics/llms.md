Large Language Models are transformer-based neural networks trained on massive text corpora to predict the next token — and it turns out that's enough to make them useful for almost anything involving language.

## Core idea

- **Pretraining** — learn general language patterns from huge, broad datasets.
- **Fine-tuning / alignment** — shape the base model toward helpful, safe behavior (RLHF, instruction tuning).
- **In-context learning** — the model adapts to a task just from examples in the prompt, no retraining.
- **Scale** — capability tends to improve predictably with more parameters, data, and compute.

## Why it matters

LLMs are the substrate under modern [NLP](#/topic/nlp), most of today's [Generative AI](#/topic/generative-ai) hype, and every [Agent](#/topic/agents) that reasons and calls tools. They also raise real [Ethics & Safety](#/topic/ethics-safety) questions around hallucination, bias, and misuse.
